01 · Seasons

When to go

East Africa straddles the equator, so there is no summer or winter. Instead there are two dry seasons and two rainy seasons, and wildlife is excellent all year.

Dry seasons

June – September · December – February

Firmer gorilla trails, animals concentrated around water, and the Mara River crossings from July to October. Peak demand: book permits and lodges 6–12 months ahead.

Green seasons

March – May · October – November

Lush landscapes, newborn animals, far fewer vehicles and lodge discounts of 15–30%. Expect afternoon downpours and muddier forest treks; April and May are wettest.

02 · Budget

What a safari costs

Private safari pricing is mostly driven by three things: the standard of lodge, how you travel between parks, and permits. Prices below are per person per night (pppn) sharing, and include accommodation, meals, a private 4x4 with your own guide, park entry and activities.

Mid-range

$250 – 400 pppn

Comfortable, characterful lodges and permanent tented camps with en-suite bathrooms, good food and great locations. Think Mahogany Springs in Bwindi or Ilkeliani Camp in the Mara.

Luxury

$600 – 1,500+ pppn

Intimate design-led lodges with private plunge pools, butler service and exclusive conservancy access, such as Clouds Mountain Gorilla Lodge, Bisate or Singita Grumeti.

03 · Entry

Visas

Most visitors need a visa for each country. Apply online before you travel; visas on arrival are increasingly being phased out and queues can be long. Your passport must be valid for at least six months after departure, with two blank pages.

$100

East Africa Tourist Visa

One visa covering Uganda, Rwanda and Kenya, valid for 90 days with multiple entry between the three. Apply through the e-visa portal of the country you enter first, and enter that country first. It does not cover Tanzania.

Uganda

Single-entry e-visa $50, 90 days. Apply at visas.immigration.go.ug. Approval usually takes 3–5 working days.

Rwanda

30-day visa for most nationalities, available online via irembo.gov.rw or on arrival at Kigali. Many African and Commonwealth citizens travel visa-free.

Kenya

Most visitors need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (eTA). Apply at least two weeks ahead at etakenya.go.ke.

Tanzania & Zanzibar

Single-entry e-visa $50 ($100 for US citizens). Apply at visa.immigration.go.tz. Zanzibar also requires mandatory inbound travel insurance.

Visa rules change often. Always confirm requirements for your nationality with the official portal or embassy before booking flights.

04 · Wellbeing

Health & insurance

See a travel clinic 6–8 weeks before departure. These are the essentials; your doctor will advise on routine vaccinations such as hepatitis A, typhoid and tetanus.

Yellow fever

Vaccination is mandatory for entry to Uganda and you must carry the yellow card (International Certificate of Vaccination). Rwanda, Kenya and Tanzania require it if you arrive from, or transit through, a yellow-fever country, which includes Uganda and Kenya. In practice: get vaccinated and keep the card with your passport.

Malaria

Present in most safari areas below about 2,000 m. Take prophylaxis as prescribed (commonly atovaquone-proguanil or doxycycline), use repellent with DEET or picaridin, and wear long sleeves at dusk. Lodges provide nets or screened rooms. Any fever within a few months of returning home should be tested promptly.

Travel insurance

Comprehensive insurance is a condition of booking with us. It must cover medical treatment, emergency air evacuation (for example by AMREF Flying Doctors), and trip cancellation, including non-refundable gorilla permits. Check it covers trekking at up to 3,000 m.

Gorilla health rules

Gorillas share about 98% of our DNA and catch human illnesses easily. If you have a cold, flu or other infectious illness on trekking day, rangers may refuse entry. Masks may be required, and the minimum age is 15.

06 · Logistics

Getting around

Choose your arrival airport based on where your safari starts. Open-jaw tickets, flying into one country and out of another, often save a full day of travel.

EBB

Entebbe, Uganda

Gateway for Bwindi, Kibale, Queen Elizabeth and Murchison. 40 minutes from Kampala via the expressway.

KGL

Kigali, Rwanda

2.5 hours to Volcanoes National Park, and 4–5 hours to southern Bwindi across the border.

NBO

Nairobi, Kenya

Jomo Kenyatta International, the region's main hub. Safari flights leave from nearby Wilson Airport.

JRO

Kilimanjaro, Tanzania

Closest international airport to Arusha, Tarangire, Ngorongoro and the Serengeti.

Domestic flights

Scheduled light aircraft link the main parks and save long drives. Aerolink flies Entebbe to Kihihi and Kisoro (for Bwindi), Pakuba (Murchison) and Kidepo. In Kenya, Safarilink and Airkenya fly from Wilson Airport to the Mara and Amboseli; in Tanzania, Coastal Aviation and Auric Air connect Arusha, the Serengeti and Zanzibar. Luggage limit: 15 kg in a soft bag.

By road

Most of our safaris use a private 4x4 Land Cruiser with a pop-up roof and your own guide. Roads are mostly good tarmac with murram sections near the parks. Traffic drives on the left in Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania, and on the right in Rwanda.

  • Entebbe → Bwindi9–10 hrs
  • Kampala → Murchison Falls5–6 hrs
  • Kigali → Volcanoes NP2.5 hrs
  • Nairobi → Masai Mara5–6 hrs
  • Arusha → Serengeti (Seronera)7–8 hrs
07 · Travel well

Responsible travel

Tourism pays for the rangers who protect gorillas and the schools beside the parks. A few simple habits make sure your visit leaves the right footprint.

Respect the wildlife

Keep at least 7 m from gorillas and chimps, never feed animals, and ask your guide to keep a respectful distance at sightings. No off-road driving where it is prohibited.

Leave no plastic

Single-use plastic bags are banned in Kenya, Rwanda and Tanzania. Bring a refillable bottle and fabric pouches.

Ask before photographing

Always ask before photographing people, and avoid photographing military sites, police and border posts.

Spend locally

Hire a porter on gorilla treks, buy crafts from community cooperatives, and visit community projects such as the Batwa experience near Bwindi.

Tip fairly

As a rough guide: $15–25 per guest per day for your driver-guide, $10–20 for gorilla rangers and trackers, and the lodge staff box at checkout.

Dress modestly in towns

Cover shoulders and knees in villages, Stone Town and religious sites. Safari clothing is perfectly fine in the parks.
